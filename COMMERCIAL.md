# Commercial collaboration

VFM-engine itself is not offered for sale as an asset. Commercial collaboration
should use clearly separated roles and agreements.

The active `main` development line offers an open-source option under
`AGPL-3.0-or-later`. An organization that wants rights outside those AGPL terms
may negotiate a separate commercial license for material the licensor controls.
No commercial rights arise merely from this page or from the contract drafts in
`commercial/`. The MIT rights in `v1.0-thesis` remain available without a new
commercial license.

| Role | What the organization receives | Platform ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial licensee | Defined rights in a named version, product, field, or deployment | No transfer |
| Services customer | PoC, integration, validation, deployment, maintenance, training, or support | No transfer |
| Development sponsor | Roadmap support and any expressly listed sponsor benefits | No transfer; no exclusivity by default |
| Corporate contributor | Ability to submit authorized contributions under DCO and a suitable CLA | Contributor retains copyright under the draft model; project receives broad licenses |

An organization may hold more than one role, but each role should be documented
separately. Payment in exchange for defined features, milestones, acceptance, or
services should be handled as paid development or services, even if the
relationship is described publicly as sponsorship. It should not be disguised
as a donation.

## Recommended ownership structure

At present, Qi Li should retain the core VFM-engine copyright. A future company
may receive a written, scoped commercialization license and operate contracting,
invoicing, licensing, support, and integration. The company's formation,
financing, or change of control should not automatically carry all existing and
future VFM-engine copyright.

## Default commercial boundary

A commercial license should be non-exclusive unless a separately negotiated,
precisely limited, and appropriately priced written clause says otherwise. A
typical license may permit the licensee to:

- use and internally reproduce the licensed software;
- integrate and compile it with a named licensed product;
- deploy it for agreed internal or hosted use; and
- distribute object-code binaries only as an embedded part of the named product.

If source access is included, the default should prohibit standalone source
redistribution, source sublicensing, publication, transfer to affiliates or
contractors outside the agreed controls, and use to create a competing
standalone VFM-engine distribution, unless expressly agreed in writing.

The following are not transferred by default:

- copyright or ownership of VFM-engine;
- the project, repository, history, brand, roadmap, or release authority;
- future versions outside the defined maintenance entitlement; or
- generic core improvements, architecture, algorithms, bug fixes, performance
  improvements, or reusable tooling.

Customer-owned data, brands, specifications, and pre-existing customer software
remain customer property. A project-specific adapter may receive separately
negotiated rights, but it must be separated from reusable VFM-engine core
improvements in the SOW.

## Revenue components

Keep these items distinct in proposals and invoices:

1. one-time PoC or integration services;
2. commercial license fees for defined product rights; and
3. recurring maintenance, upgrades, and support.

Per-device, per-deployment, annual, or project pricing may be evaluated only
after the customer's product scope and distribution model are known.

## Draft documents

The [`commercial/`](commercial/) directory contains planning drafts for a
commercial license, services SOW, development sponsorship, individual CLA, and
corporate CLA. Every draft is marked not for signature. A qualified Chinese
intellectual-property lawyer should review the relevant documents, authority of
the signing parties, tax treatment, employment/institutional restrictions,
export controls, data obligations, and governing-law provisions before use.
