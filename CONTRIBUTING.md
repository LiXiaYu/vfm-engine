# Contributing to VFM-engine

Thank you for considering a contribution. Before opening a pull request, read
the [contribution policy](docs/CONTRIBUTION_POLICY.md), [provenance record](PROVENANCE.md),
and [licensing policy](LICENSING.md).

## Required sign-off

Every contribution and every commit must certify the unmodified Developer
Certificate of Origin 1.1 in [DCO](DCO). Add a sign-off with:

```text
git commit -s
```

This adds a line in the following form, using your real identity:

```text
Signed-off-by: Full Name <email@example.com>
```

The sign-off is a certification, not merely formatting. Do not sign for code or
other material you do not have the right to submit.

## Before submitting

You must not contribute:

- code or other material owned by your employer, university, institute,
  customer, funder, or another person unless you have written authorization;
- confidential, unpublished, export-controlled, privacy-restricted, or trade
  secret information belonging to any organization or person;
- third-party material that you cannot lawfully relicense under the applicable
  project terms; or
- material subject to a conflicting patent, contract, policy, or funding term.

If the contribution was developed in the course of employment, appointment,
consulting, sponsored research, or use of material institutional resources,
provide the maintainer with the relevant written authorization before review.

## CLA requirement

DCO sign-off is required for all contributions. A Contributor License Agreement
may additionally be required before accepting contributions to a core intended
for open-source and commercial dual licensing. Contributors retain copyright
under the draft CLA model while granting the project steward sufficient
copyright and patent rights to distribute the contribution under open-source
and commercial terms.

Do not treat the templates in `commercial/` as executed agreements. The project
will identify the agreement and signature process applicable to a real
contribution.

## Pull-request record

For a material feature or refactor, state:

- what changed and why;
- tests and validation performed;
- provenance category from `PROVENANCE.md`;
- third-party code, data, specifications, or tools used;
- employer/institution/funder involvement and written clearance, if any; and
- whether the change affects public APIs, licensing, security, or compatibility.

Keep commits reviewable and include the DCO sign-off in every commit.
