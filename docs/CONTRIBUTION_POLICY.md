# Contribution policy

## Purpose

This policy protects contributors, employers and institutions, users, and the
VFM-engine project by requiring a documented right to submit every contribution.

## Mandatory DCO certification

Every commit must contain a `Signed-off-by` line matching the contributor's real
identity and certifying the unmodified DCO 1.1 in the repository root. A pull
request with missing or invalid sign-offs must not merge until corrected without
rewriting other contributors' work improperly.

## Restricted material

Do not submit material that:

- is owned or controlled by an employer, university, institute, laboratory,
  customer, sponsor, or funder without written permission;
- contains confidential, non-public, personal, clinical, security-sensitive,
  export-controlled, or trade secret information;
- was copied or derived from a third party without a compatible license and the
  right to submit it under the project's applicable terms;
- is subject to a patent or contractual restriction inconsistent with the
  intended open-source and commercial licensing; or
- was produced using accounts, data, tools, facilities, or funds whose terms
  impose unrecorded ownership or disclosure restrictions.

Public availability is not proof of an open-source license. A citation is not a
substitute for permission to copy code.

## Employment and institutional clearance

Contributors working under employment, sponsored research, consultancy, or
another institutional relationship must check the relevant IP, publication,
confidentiality, funding, and software policies. When the relationship could
reasonably claim the contribution, obtain written authorization from a person
with authority to bind the rightsholder.

A supervisor's informal approval may be useful evidence but may not bind the
employer or institution.

## Provenance record for material changes

The review record should identify:

- contributor and sign-off;
- origin category defined in `PROVENANCE.md`;
- relevant employer, institution, customer, or funder;
- third-party sources and exact licenses;
- automated or generative tools materially used;
- written clearance or agreement reference, where needed; and
- files or subsystems affected.

The maintainer may request additional evidence or reject a contribution when
rights cannot be verified.

## CLA

For copyrightable material intended for the dual-licensed codebase, the project
requires an executed individual or corporate CLA in addition to DCO whenever
the contributor is not the applicable project licensor. The CLA process must
identify the actual signing party, authority, approved agreement version, and
signature record. It cannot be replaced by committing an unsigned template,
opening a pull request, or adding a DCO sign-off.

Until the required CLA is executed, external work may be discussed and reviewed
but must not be merged. The maintainer may reject any contribution whose
copyright, patent, employer, institution, or funding position is unclear.
